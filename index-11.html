Good to know in my Roman home

A few practical things I’d tell a friend visiting Rome for the first time — especially if you’re coming from the United States.

Electricity
Italy uses 230 V

Italian sockets commonly use Type C, F and L plugs. A travel adapter changes the plug shape, but it does not convert voltage. Before plugging in a US appliance, check that its label says 100–240 V. Please never force a plug into a socket.

Power at home
One high-power appliance at a time

Homes in Italy often have lower electrical capacity than US homes. Please avoid running several high-power appliances together — for example A/C + stove + hairdryer — as this can trip the power.

Water
Rome’s tap water is drinkable

You can drink the tap water. Around Rome you’ll also see the small public drinking fountains known as nasoni — perfect for refilling your bottle.

Hot water
The water heater has a tank

Hot water is not unlimited. Please leave the middle bathroom switch ON and allow the electric water heater time to recover after several long showers.

Before you head out
My 20-second home checklist

Take your keys and phone · turn off A/C or heating · turn off the stove, kitchen appliances and lights · never leave an appliance running unattended · close the apartment door and the building entrance behind you.

Tipping in Italy is appreciated for excellent service but it is not expected at US percentages. A restaurant’s coperto is a cover charge, not a tip. Cards are widely accepted. If a card terminal or ATM asks whether to charge you in USD or EUR, choosing EUR generally lets your own card network handle the conversion instead of the merchant’s dynamic currency conversion.

Public transport
Metro & bus made simple

The current ATAC BIT ticket is €1.50 and is valid for 100 minutes. You can also use contactless Tap & Go where available. If you use a paper ticket, validate it as required and keep it until the end of your journey.

Rome without surprises
A few local habits

Rome is best explored on foot, but watch for scooters and traffic when crossing. In crowded tourist areas and on public transport, keep your phone and wallet secure as you would in any major city. Carry a refillable bottle for the nasoni. Pharmacies are marked by a green cross; for a medical emergency call 112 or 118.
